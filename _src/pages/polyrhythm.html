Polyrhythm

One beat of Xenakis, taken apart. Measure 21 of Rebonds a sets five against three and hides a triplet in the middle. Press play, watch every stroke land as a bubble, and build the beat back up one layer at a time.

The whole beat

The first beat of measure 21 is one of the knots in Rebonds a. After a single sixteenth on the high bongo, two voices share the rest of the beat. The upper voice plays five even notes in the time of three sixteenths. Under it, the lower voice plays those same three sixteenths as two notes, a triplet, and two notes.

That is eleven attacks in about a second at the printed tempo, and the space between them changes every single time. The way in is to stop hearing eleven attacks and start hearing three sixteenths, each with its own small shape.

Each bubble is as wide as its note is long. Five equal bubbles sit on the line; two, three, and two hang under it, and the two rows fill the same space. Wherever two bubbles meet, a stroke lands. Tap any bubble to hear its drum.

Work through the eight steps in order, slowly, and come back to the first one often. Take turns plays the beat, then leaves the same space for you to play it back. Upper notes sound slightly to the right and lower notes slightly to the left, so headphones help.

The drums in this beat

Rebonds a is for seven skins: two bongos, three tom-toms, and two bass drums, tuned over a very wide range. Six of them play here; the low bass drum sits this beat out. Tap one to hear it.

    How the beat is built

    Four ideas hold the whole thing together. Each one is a step on the page above.

    Five against three

    Count each sixteenth in five, and the upper voice takes every third count: 1 and 4, then 2 and 5, then 3. That is the whole polyrhythm. The lower voice only fills in around it.

    Two, three, two

    The lower voice keeps the three sixteenths in plain sight: two thirty-seconds, a thirty-second triplet, two thirty-seconds. The first note of each group is one sixteenth of the frame.

    Why ninety

    The five cuts the dotted eighth into fifths. The lower voice cuts it into sixths and ninths. The smallest ruler that holds all three has 90 marks, so every stroke here is counted in ninetieths. At quarter note = 40, one mark is 12.5 milliseconds.

    The mirror

    The beat reads the same in both directions. The first sixteenth has a flam led by the lower voice, the third has a flam led by the upper voice, and the middle sixteenth is its own mirror. Learn the first half well and you already know the second.

    Now put it back in the piece

    One beat is a study, not a performance. Play measure 21 from your own score, at a tempo where the five stays even, and let the beats on either side tell you where it sits. Rebonds (1987–89) by Iannis Xenakis is published by Éditions Salabert; this page quotes a single beat for study.
