The story so far

Twenty-five years behind the drums, told in photographs and concert posters: from Southern California and Reno to a home studio in Hermiston, Oregon, and the concert stage at Angelo State.

{{keys}}

1983–2001 · California, Nevada, Oregon

Three hometowns, then a studio

Trent Shuey was born in North Hollywood, California, in 1983, and North Hollywood was home first. Then came Thousand Oaks, in the hills northwest of Los Angeles; then Reno, Nevada; and then, in his teens, Hermiston, a farm town on the Columbia River in eastern Oregon. Each place kept a claim on him: he would go back to Reno for graduate school, and Hermiston is still where his family is anchored. By the time he was out of high school the local paper had taken notice. “A different drummer… the man for all styles,” the Hermiston Herald wrote in 2000.

The photo is from the room where the drumming got serious: a blue drum set under a ceiling of cymbals, microphones set up, friends plugging in guitars. Most of what came later, from jazz clubs to orchestra pits to marching fields, can be traced back to afternoons in this room.

2006–2009 · First students, first degrees

Teaching before finishing school

He taught percussion at Hermiston High School from 2006 to 2008 while studying with Margaret Mayer at Blue Mountain Community College, where he earned an Associate of Arts in 2007. A year later BMCC hired him back as Director of Percussion Studies.

At Eastern Oregon University he studied with Teun Fetz and finished a Bachelor of Music magna cum laude in 2009. That same year he stepped in front of the Grande Ronde Symphony as timpani soloist for the world premiere of a concerto by Kvistad, the first of many turns in the orchestra, including principal chairs with the Grande Ronde Symphony and the Inland Northwest Symphony.

2009–2012 · Reno, Nevada

Jazz in the high desert

A graduate teaching assistantship took him back to Reno, the city he had lived in as a kid, and to the University of Nevada, where he studied with Andrew Heglund and Cody Remaklus and finished a Master of Music with a minor in jazz in 2012. In 2011 the Reno Jazz Festival named him Outstanding Drum Set Soloist.

Reno was also where the range widened: steel drums with the UNR I-Pan ensemble, summers on the faculty of the Lake Tahoe Music Camp, principal percussion and timpani with the Tahoe Symphony, and concerts with the Reno Philharmonic and the Reno Chamber Orchestra.

2012–2015 · Urbana-Champaign

Illinois and the marimba

Doctoral study at the University of Illinois meant years with William Moersch, one of the marimba's great commissioners, and with Ricardo Flores. Trent won the university's Edgard Varèse Award in 2013, taught as a graduate assistant, worked summers at Illinois Summer Youth Music, and played with the Champaign-Urbana Symphony and Sinfonia da Camera.

His dissertation became a performance guide to five marimba works commissioned by Moersch, and the recital that went with it carried three world premieres. The recordings are on SoundCloud.

2015 · San Angelo, Texas

Angelo State

In 2015 Trent joined the faculty of Angelo State University as Assistant Director of Bands and percussion instructor. He directed the Symphonic Band for his first three years, then moved to the jazz program, and he has written every battery arrangement the Ram Band Drumline has played since, more than 40 halftime shows so far.

The studio he inherited had four percussion students. It has grown to sixteen, and the drumline has gone from 8 players to more than 20. Along the way he created the Ram Horns pep band identity in 2016, founded the ASU Day of Percussion in 2017, and secured a $25,000 Rea Charitable Trust grant for new instruments.

2018–2019 · Season posters

Every concert gets a poster

2018–2021 · Yucatán, Mexico

ÓperaMaya

Starting in 2018, summers meant the ÓperaMaya International Music Festival in the Yucatán: six seasons in all, eventually on the percussion faculty, with timpani set up inside a cave for a concert in 2021. The festival's percussion scholarship came his way in 2019 and again in 2021, and the trips are part of a performing résumé that spans the United States, Mexico, and Brazil.

2020 · Doctor of Musical Arts

The degree, and a premiere with his name on it

The doctorate from Illinois was completed in 2020. The same year he gave the world premiere of William Kang's Shuey Brew, Coffee Stains, an Office, and the Spine, performing alongside the composer.

2021 · Hermiston

Paul

In July 2021 Trent lost his father, Paul Thomas Shuey, a radiographer, an expert-rated chess player, and a camper and fisherman. Paul's fish painting is the reason this website looks the way it does: its colors and brushstrokes are painted under every page title. Read the memorial page.

2021–2022 · Back on stage

Big band, percussion ensemble, and the first combo nights

Concerts came back. The Big Band played a lunchtime blues set under the blue canopy, paid tribute to Sammy Nestico, and premiered El Mambo! in December 2021. The Percussion Ensemble played Magic that November and Rides, Games, and Trickery the next spring. The jazz combos started playing out: the first Jazz Combo Night at Back Beat Music Company in November 2021, then a night at the Cactus Hotel with the trumpet ensembles the following spring. His own teacher Teun Fetz came to the 2022 Day of Percussion, and August 2022 was spent on the field with the drumline.

Fort Concho

The Official Drummer

San Angelo grew up around a frontier army post, and the post is still there. As the Official Drummer of Fort Concho, Trent plays an original 1867 drum at living history events, giving visitors the sound of military music on the Texas frontier. More about Fort Concho.

2023 · Recital, parade, and a home at FiFi's

A full year, and a home downtown

In February 2023 the jazz combos moved into The House of FiFi DuBois on Chadbourne Street, and it has been home ever since: combo nights, Saturday concerts, and the open jam sessions that follow them, all under the neon and the Wild West Show sign. The same year brought a faculty recital at the marimba, the Ram Band on parade, the Big Band at the Blue Buffalo Art Gallery and a Swingin' Christmas at the Brooks and Bates Theatre, and the Percussion Ensemble's Fiesta Latina with the brass ensembles.

2024 · Latin Jazz

The big band finds its groove

A spring program of big band history gave way to a fall of Latin jazz, with the ASU Jazz Ensemble on stage in October 2024. The jazz program now includes the big band and four combos.

2025–2026 · Philip Glass

Two timpanists, one concerto

In September 2025 he and Dr. Lisa Rogers of Texas Tech were the soloists in Philip Glass's Concerto Fantasy for Two Timpanists with the ASU Symphony Orchestra, on a program billed as Thunderstruck. In February 2026 the two of them played it again with the Big Spring Symphony and its conductor, Dr. Fagner Rocha.

Fall 2025 · FiFi's, Indianapolis, and Glimmer

A September at FiFi's, a November in Indianapolis

The jazz combos opened the fall at The House of FiFi DuBois on September 13, a concert at four and an open jam at five, the pattern the combos keep to this day. November meant the Percussive Arts Society convention in Indianapolis, and the Percussion Ensemble closed the semester with Glimmer.

2026 · The biggest year yet

From Big Spring to FiFi's to In C

After the Glass concerto in Big Spring in February came the Percussion Ensemble's House Music in April and bongos and mambo bell for Tito Puente Jr. on Mother's Day, which he calls the biggest gig of his life. Then the 49th ASU Summer Music Camp in June, band camp in August, and the combos back at The House of FiFi DuBois in September and again for Halloween, in costume. In between came section percussion with the West Texas Symphony and a Jazz Ensemble tribute to Chick Corea in October, and Terry Riley's In C with ASU music faculty and students on November 3.
