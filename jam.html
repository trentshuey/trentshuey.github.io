Jam Session 101

What is a jam session?

A jam session is where jazz musicians can come and “sit in” with the house band. Historically, this is how jazz musicians discovered each other and how they advertised themselves. Jam sessions were also a place where younger players could absorb and apply what the older players were doing, as well as experiment with their own material.

Jam sessions are made possible through the shared body of jazz standards and the general knowledge of how jazz is performed and how it is structured.

How does a jam session work?

The house band will normally play a tune or even a full set.

Those who arrive can put their name down on a list at the bandstand and note what tune they would like to play. The tune should be a jazz standard or something in any provided fakebooks. Otherwise, the musician should bring their own charts.

Your name will be called and you go up to the bandstand to play with the band. More than one person can go up at any given time.

What do I need to bring?

Your instrument and anything you need to play. If there is no guitar player in the house band, bring your own amplifier. Bring your own sticks and anything else you need to perform. Music stands are normally provided.

If you play a bass clef instrument, it is wise to bring your own fakebook or iPad. Bring anything you need to be successful.

Jam session etiquette

Generally, musicians only play 2–4 choruses on a given tune, depending on the length of the tune. This is a performance, but you want to make room for others. No hogging the stage. Take cues from those who play before you about how long you should play!

How can I prepare?

Come prepared with a few jazz standards that you can call. Ideally, these are tunes you can play without music in front of you.

The number one piece of advice for a jam session is to only play tunes you are fully prepared to play. Know what key your tune is in to avoid any confusion. Have iReal Pro or a fakebook that you are familiar with if you intend to sit in on other people’s tunes.
